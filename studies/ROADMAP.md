# Proposed learning sequence

Adapt this plan to observed progress. Full-time availability supports regular study;
it does not establish an exam-ready date. No stage below is completed yet.

This is the initial cross-subject direction, not a fixed curriculum for every subject.
Use [SUBJECTS.md](SUBJECTS.md) and each subject's canonical plan when switching tracks;
add requested subjects with their actual requirements and prerequisite evidence.

| Stage | Material | Evidence to collect before advancing |
|---|---|---|
| Computer foundations | Information representation, programs, operating systems, files, permissions and basic Python | Explain the model, perform simple tasks and solve unfamiliar beginner exercises |
| Networking foundations | Packets, addressing, DNS, routing, transport protocols and traffic observation | Trace a communication example and explain observed behaviour |
| Security+ track | Confirmed official objectives, security concepts, threats, identity, cryptography, architecture, operations and risk | Source-backed explanations, reviewed practice questions and demonstrated application across objectives |
| Exam preparation | Scenario questions, practical tasks, timed practice and targeted review | Recorded results on validated questions, coverage gaps and repeated misconceptions |
| Engineering depth | Mathematics/CS foundations, embedded systems, firmware, industrial networks and manufacturing security alongside EE study | Documented projects, measurements, independent review and deeper technical study |

Teach code from scratch and build the sandbox progression in `LABS.md` alongside
the relevant topics, using `PROCEDURES.md`. The longer-term AI-assisted checking
system is a progressive build goal. Exam readiness and production readiness each
require their own recorded evidence.

Computer science foundations will be studied as skills needed for the goal. The
owner's intended formal degree is electrical engineering. Security+ preparation
and later expert-level engineering are separate milestones with their own evidence.

## Session rhythm

1. Resume from the hub `STATE.md` and active subject's STATE; review recorded gaps or due material.
2. Inspect selected sources and teach one manageable concept from first principles.
3. Perform a demonstration or exercise and ask the learner to explain the result.
4. Use reviewed questions to test transfer; record real responses and misconceptions.
5. Update the state and log, including the next concrete session action.

Choose exact daily timing with the owner when sessions begin. Build and validate
the question bank progressively; confirm the exam version before objective mapping.
