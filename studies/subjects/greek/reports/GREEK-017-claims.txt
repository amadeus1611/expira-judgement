Contract verbs are verbs whose stems end in alpha, epsilon, or omicron.
That final vowel is called the "contract vowel."
When that final stem vowel comes into contact with the connecting vowel, the two vowels contract.
All alpha contracts behave similarly, as do all epsilon and all omicron contracts.
We meet contractions only in two tenses, the present and the imperfect.
ου is formed from εο, οε, and oo.
ω is formed from almost any combination of omicron or omega with any other vowel, except for rule #1.
If the contract vowel and the first vowel of the diphthong are the same, they simplify.
If the second vowel of the diphthong is an iota, it subscripts if possible; if it is an upsilon it drops off.
There always will be a circumflex over the contracted vowels in the present indicative.
Since the personal ending begins with tau, the connecting vowel must be an epsilon.
